# Experience

## ByteNovators — Web Developer
I worked at ByteNovators on client-facing web projects, with much of my day-to-day work centered on React/Next.js applications rather than a narrowly defined frontend product team. I also heavily contributed to and refactored the .NET backend for their CRM. One of the more substantial pieces of work was for Trek De Kashmir: I built a quote/lead form that connected the public site to a lead API, worked on package and itinerary functionality, added a Google Ads conversion flow through a `/thanks` route, and handled SEO-oriented content and metadata. I also worked on a Next.js + Sanity blog using incremental static regeneration. The interesting part was not any single component; it was having to connect the user-facing flow to backend endpoints, content management, analytics/conversion requirements, and deployment constraints. I also learned what happens when a small organization has very little engineering structure: requirements and priorities could be unclear, so I often had to make implementation decisions with limited specification and take responsibility for getting the work into a usable state. I worked there through August 2026, after which I resigned and am currently looking for my next role.

## Chinar Quantum AI — Frontend Development Intern
At Chinar Quantum AI, I worked as part of a small team where I owned much of the administrative frontend for the company website. I built interfaces for job postings, applications, and announcements, including CRUD flows, tables, forms, charts, validation, caching, and data fetching. The stack was Next.js and TypeScript with SWR and Zod, with Sanity CMS in a TurboRepo monorepo. I also touched server actions/API handlers, database-related logic, and input validation rather than treating the browser as the entire application. A useful engineering challenge was keeping fairly data-heavy admin screens predictable: validation, asynchronous fetching, cache invalidation, and UI state all had to agree when records were created or edited. I also participated in discussions around an ML ERP chatbot and its architecture, which pushed me to think beyond individual frontend components and about how application layers fit together.

## Faveo

There I learnt Vue.js, Bootstrap, axios, state management with Vuex, form handling and other things around the Vue ecosystem. I had fun there and learnt a lot in a very short period of time. After completion, they offered me a Junior SDE role, but I had to decline because I couldn't move out of Kashmir back then. Their HR was rather nice to me.

## NIT Srinagar — Machine Learning Training
I completed a short machine-learning training program at NIT Srinagar, under Dr Janib Bashir, where I was introduced to practical ML workflows and the surrounding tooling - scikit-learn, CNNs for image recognition and classification. It was an early exploration of machine learning rather than professional ML engineering. Its main value in my trajectory was that it exposed me to a different side of software development and helped me explore whether ML was the direction I wanted to pursue. I ultimately found myself more engaged by application development and engineering systems, which is why my subsequent work shifted toward web development and, increasingly, backend engineering. During the training I made image classifiers for the MNIST datasets, a random collection of cat and dog pictures I got from the web, and then a number of small GANs to generate images. I put the learning and work with GANs into Google Collab Notebooks and made a GitHub repository of them. I wrote explanations and example in them and thought it could help people who wanted to learn about GANs.
