# FAQ

## Are you open to work?
Yes. I resigned from ByteNovators in August 2026 and am currently open to new opportunities.

## Why full-stack, not just frontend?
Frontend is currently my strongest practical area, but I do not want my engineering responsibility to stop at the browser. I am more interested in understanding and owning the complete path from user interaction to API, business logic, database, and deployment. My frontend experience gives me a useful base; I am now deliberately building backend depth with .NET, C#, ASP.NET Core, Node, gRPC, PostgreSQL, Entity Framework Core, and related systems concepts.

## What are you looking for next?
I am looking for a full-stack or backend-leaning software engineering role where I can work on real product problems and gradually take ownership of larger parts of a system. I want an environment where engineering decisions matter: API design, data modeling, testing, architecture, reliability, and deployment, not just UI implementation. I am particularly interested in teams where I can use my existing Next.js/TypeScript skills while growing substantially on the backend.

## What is your strongest skill right now?
Modern web application development, especially React/Next.js with TypeScript. I have used that stack across public-facing sites, admin applications, API-backed forms, CMS integrations, SEO work, server-side functionality, caching, and deployment-related work. It is the area where I currently have the most hands-on experience.

## What is a project you're proudest of and why?
The TravelByte CRM I worked on for ByteNovators is one of the more complex products I was involved with. I was heavily contributing to it from UIs to backend APIs, handlers and models. Their requirements were very vague, and I ended up using my own judgement more often than not. The domain the product was targetting to is also very unorganized, and that made it very difficult to think about how the data would flow and be processed, how the database should keep it, how UX can be made pleasing. In the end, I ended up owning and contributing over a dozen features to the product, end to end, in a messy environment, and I think that's something to be proud of.

ColorWars is also important to me for a different reason. It is unfinished, but it forces me to learn real-time architecture, WebSockets, matchmaking, connection state, and authoritative server logic. I would rather describe that honestly as an in-progress engineering project than claim it is finished. I wrote it in Java, and it is very basic, but two people can play a game over WebSockets. It is a fun side project I tried and had a lot of fun with. I also learnt a lot of Java along the way.
